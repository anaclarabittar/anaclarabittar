About

It started with marketing, then moved to the people and processes behind it.

I studied Marketing and Advertising in Brazil and started out in social media. Over the next few years I moved closer to clients: customer success, partner management, and then account management for creators and brands.

Along the way I noticed that the part I liked most wasn't the meetings. It was building the systems that keep work moving: the calendar that flags a gap early, the approval flow that stops a post from shipping too soon, the automation that removes a step people kept forgetting.

Today I live in Portugal and run content operations for B2B clients at a marketing agency, mostly async, through Slack and Notion.

Content operations

Content operations & approval system for a multi-client B2B portfolio

Scaled without a single dropped deadline
+

With many accounts running in parallel, content could easily get stuck waiting on a visual, needing a shorter version for another platform, or falling off the calendar until the deadline was close.

What I did

  • Ran a recurring calendar audit across every active account to flag gaps early
  • Kept a daily, per-client breakdown of what was blocked and why
  • Set up a two-stage flow: content stays internal until approved, then moves to the client-facing calendar
  • Checked every post before it went live: visuals matched the copy, tags were right, sensitive details hidden
Process & automation

Turning a manual onboarding step into an automated workflow

Built on my own initiative, not requested
+

Each new client needed a standard set of tracking documents created by hand. There was no clear trigger for "a new client has arrived," so the step was often missed until a deadline was close.

What I did

  • Mapped the existing process and found where it broke down
  • Proposed a form-triggered workflow: submitting client details creates the documents and notifies the team in Slack
  • Built the case and presented it proactively

Onboarding no longer depends on someone remembering to do it.

How I work

Async first, written down, and early rather than urgent.

Written over live

Updates, decisions and next steps go in Slack or Notion, where anyone can find them later. Calls are for the conversations that need one.

Flag it early

A gap in the calendar on Monday is easy to fix. The same gap on Friday is a problem. I look ahead so you don't have to.

Systems over memory

If a step depends on someone remembering it, it will eventually be missed. I turn those steps into checklists, templates or automations.

Flexible across time zones

Based in Portugal and fully fluent in English. I keep flexible hours and cover reasonable overlap with US or EU teams.

When everyone can see where the work stands, the work gets lighter.
